## Removal of cylinder head

(See page EM-97)

1.  Disconnect cable from negative terminal of battery.

    !!! warning "Caution"

        Work must be started after approx. 20 seconds or longer from the time the ignition switch is turned to the
        `LOCK` position and the negative (`–`) terminal cable is disconnected from the battery.

2.  Drain engine coolant. (See page CO-6)
3.  Remove engine under covers.
4.  Remove engine hood side panels.
5.  Remove suspension upper brace.
6.  (A/T) Disconnect throttle cable from throttle body.
7.  Disconnect accelerator cable from throttle body.
8.  Remove cruise control actuator (w/ cruise control system) and accelerator linkage. (See step 11 on page EM-182)
9.  Remove air cleaner cap. (See step 9 on page EM-182)
10. Disconnect ground strap connector.

33. Remove camshafts.

    !!! note "Notice"

        Since the thrust clearance of the camshaft is small, the camshaft must be kept level while it is being
        removed. If the camshaft is not kept level, the portion of the cylinder head receiving the shaft thrust may
        crack or be damaged, causing the camshaft to seize or break. To avoid this, the following steps should be
        carried out.

    **A. Remove exhaust camshaft**

    1.  Set the knock pin of the intake camshaft at 10 – 45° BTDC of camshaft angle.

        !!! tip "Hint"

            The above angle allows No.2 and No.4 cylinder cam lobes of the exhaust camshaft to push their valve
            lifters evenly.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3242.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    2.  [](){ #p-em-105 } Secure the exhaust camshaft sub-gear to drive gear with a service bolt.

        **Recommended service bolt:**

        * Thread diameter – 6 mm
        * Thread pitch – 1.0 mm
        * Bolt length – 16 – 20 mm (0.63 – 0.79 in.)

        !!! tip "Hint"

            When removing the camshaft, make certain that the torsional spring force of the sub-gear has been
            eliminated by the above operation.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3243.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    3.  Remove the two bolts and rear bearing cap.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3513.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    4.  Uniformly loosen and remove the six bolts on the No.1, No.2 and No.4 bearing cap in several passes in the
        sequence shown.

        !!! note "Notice"

            Do not remove the No.3 bearing cap bolts at this stage.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3514.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    5.  Remove the No.1, No.2 and No.4 bearing caps.
    6.  Alternately loosen and remove the two bolts on the No.3 bearing cap.

        !!! tip "Hint"

            * As two No.3 bearing cap bolts are loosened, check that the camshaft is being lifted out straight and
              level.
            * If the camshaft is not being lifted out straight and level, retighten the two No.3 bearing cap bolts.
              Then reverse the order of above steps from (f) to (a) and reset the knock pin of the intake camshaft at
              10 – 45° BTDC, and repeat steps from (b) to (f) once again.

        !!! note "Notice"

            Do not pry on or attempt to force the camshaft with a tool or other objects.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3515.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    7.  Remove the No.3 bearing cap and exhaust camshaft.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3516.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    **B. Remove intake camshaft**

    1.  Set the knock pin of the intake camshaft at 80 – 115° BTDC of camshaft angle.

        !!! tip "Hint"

            The above angle allows the No.1 and No.3 cylinder cam lobes of intake camshaft to push their valve
            lifters evenly.

    2.  [](){ #p-em-106 } Remove the two bolts, front bearing cap and oil seal.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3517.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    3.  Uniformly loosen and remove the bolts on the No.1, No.3 and No.4 bearing cap in several passes in the
        sequence shown.

        !!! note "Notice"

            Do not remove the No.2 bearing cap bolts at this stage.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3518.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    4.  Remove the No.1, No.3 and No.4 bearing caps.
    5.  Alternately loosen and remove the two bolts on the No.2 bearing cap.

        !!! tip "Hint"

            * As two No.2 bearing cap bolts are loosened, check that the camshaft is being lifted out straight and
              level, after breaking the adhesion on the front bearing cap.
            * If the camshaft is not being lifted out straight and level, retighten the two No.2 bearing cap bolts.
              Reverse the order of above steps from (e) to (a) and reset the knock pin of the intake camshaft at
              80 – 115° BTDC, and repeat steps from (b) to (e) once again.

        !!! note "Notice"

            Do not pry on or attempt to force the camshaft with a tool or other objects.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3519.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    6.  Remove the No.2 bearing cap and camshaft.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3222.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

34. Disassemble exhaust camshaft.

    1.  Mount the hexagonal wrench head portion of the camshaft in a vise.

        !!! note "Notice"

            Be careful not to damage the camshaft.

    2.  Insert a service bolt (A) into the service hole of the camshaft sub-gear.
    3.  Using a screwdriver, turn the sub-gear clockwise, and remove the service bolt (B).

        !!! note "Notice"

            Be careful not to damage the camshaft.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3223.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    4.  [](){ #p-em-107 } Using snap ring pliers, remove the snap ring.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3224.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    5.  Remove the following parts:

        1. Wave washer
        2. Camshaft sub-gear
        3. Camshaft gear spring

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3225.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

35. Remove cylinder head.

    1.  Using SST, uniformly loosen and remove the ten cylinder head bolts in several passes in the sequence shown.

        **SST** `09011-38121`

        !!! note "Notice"

            Head warpage or cracking could result from removing bolts in the incorrect order.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM7557_EM7558.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    2.  Lift the cylinder head from the dowels on the cylinder block, and place the cylinder head on wooden blocks
        on a bench.

        !!! tip "Hint"

            If the cylinder head is difficult to lift off, pry between the cylinder head and cylinder block with a
            screwdriver.

        !!! note "Notice"

            Be careful not to damage the cylinder head and cylinder block surfaces of the cylinder head.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM8961.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

## Inspection, cleaning and repair of cylinder head components

1.  Clean top surfaces of pistons and cylinder block.

    1.  Turn the crankshaft, and bring each piston to top dead center (TDC). Using a gasket scraper, remove all the
        carbon from the piston top surface.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM8559.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    2.  Using a gasket scraper, remove all the gasket material from the top surface of the cylinder block.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM8558.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    3.  Using compressed air, blow carbon and oil from the bolt holes.

        !!! warning "Caution"

            Protect your eyes when using high-compressed air.

2.  Clean cylinder head.

    1.  **Remove gasket material**

        Using a gasket scraper, remove all the gasket material from the cylinder block surface.

        !!! note "Notice"

            Be careful not to scratch the cylinder block contact surface.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3304.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    2.  **Clean combustion chambers**

        Using a wire brush, remove all the carbon from the combustion chambers.

        !!! note "Notice"

            Be careful not to scratch the cylinder block contact surface.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3302.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    3.  **Clean valve guide bushings**

        Using a valve guide bushing brush and solvent, clean all the guide bushings.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3364.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    4.  [](){ #p-em-110 } **Clean cylinder head**

        Using a soft brush and solvent, thoroughly clean the cylinder head.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3301.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

3.  Inspect cylinder head.

    1.  **Inspect for flatness**

        Using precision straight edge and feeler gauge, measure the surfaces contacting the cylinder block manifolds
        for warpage.

        | Surface | Maximum warpage |
        |---|---|
        | Cylinder block side | 0.05 mm (0.0020 in.) |
        | Manifold side | 0.08 mm (0.0031 in.) |

        If warpage is greater than maximum, replace the cylinder head.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3318_EM3319_EM3320.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    2.  **Inspect for cracks**

        Using a dye penetrant, check the combustion chamber, intake ports, exhaust ports and cylinder block surface
        for cracks.

        If cracked, replace the cylinder head.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3321.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

4.  Clean valves.

    1.  Using a gasket scraper, chip off any carbon from the valve head.
    2.  Using a wire brush, thoroughly clean the valve.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM0580.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

5.  [](){ #p-em-111 } Inspect valve stems and guide bushings.

    1.  Using a caliper gauge, measure the inside diameter of the guide bushing.

        **Bushing inside diameter:** 6.010 – 6.030 mm (0.2366 – 0.2374 in.)

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3365_EM3366.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    2.  Using a micrometer, measure the diameter of the valve stem.

        **Valve stem diameter:**

        * Intake – 5.970 – 5.985 mm (0.2350 – 0.2356 in.)
        * Exhaust – 5.965 – 5.980 mm (0.2348 – 0.2354 in.)

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM0963_EM0964.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    3.  Subtract the valve stem diameter measurement from the guide bushing inside diameter measurement.

        **Standard oil clearance:**

        * Intake – 0.025 – 0.060 mm (0.0010 – 0.0024 in.)
        * Exhaust – 0.030 – 0.065 mm (0.0012 – 0.0026 in.)

        **Maximum oil clearance:**

        * Intake – 0.08 mm (0.0031 in.)
        * Exhaust – 0.10 mm (0.0039 in.)

        If the clearance is greater than maximum, replace the valve and guide bushing.

6.  If necessary, replace valve guide bushings.

    1.  (w/ Snap Ring)

        Insert an old valve wrapped with tape into the valve guide bushing, and break off the valve guide bushing by
        hitting it with a hammer. Remove the snap ring.

        !!! tip "Hint"

            Wrap the tape approx. 8 mm (0.31 in.) from the valve stem end.

        !!! note "Notice"

            Be careful not damage to the valve lifter hole.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM5335.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    2.  Gradually heat the cylinder head to 80 – 100°C (176 – 212°F).

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3345.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    3.  [](){ #p-em-112 } Using SST and a hammer, tap out the guide bushing.

        **SST** `09201-70010`

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3367.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    4.  Using a caliper gauge, measure the bushing bore diameter of the cylinder head.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3305.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    5.  Select a new guide bushing (STD size or O/S 0.05).

        Both intake and exhaust

        | Bushing bore diameter mm (in.) | Bushing size |
        |---|---|
        | 11.000 – 11.027 (0.4331 – 0.4342) | Use STD |
        | 11.050 – 11.077 (0.4350 – 0.4361) | Use O/S 0.05 |

        If the bushing bore diameter of the cylinder head is greater than 11.027 mm (0.4341 in.), machine the bushing
        bore to the following dimension:

        11.050 – 11.077 mm (0.4350 – 0.4361 in.)

        If the bushing bore diameter of the cylinder head is greater than 11.077 mm (0.4361 in.), replace the cylinder
        head.

    6.  Gradually heat the cylinder head to 80 – 100°C (176 – 212°F).
    7.  Using SST and a hammer, tap in a new guide bushing until the snap ring makes contact with the cylinder head.

        **SST** `09201-70010`

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM7825.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    8.  [](){ #p-em-113 } Using a sharp 6 mm reamer, ream the guide bushing to obtain the standard specified clearance
        (See page EM-111) between the guide bushing and valve stem.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3369_EM3370.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

7.  Inspect and grind valves.

    1.  Grind the valve enough to remove pits and carbon.
    2.  Check that the valve is ground to the correct valve face angle.

        **Valve face angle:** 44.5°

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM0254_EM0180.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    3.  Check the valve head margin thickness.

        **Standard margin thickness:** 0.8 – 1.2 mm (0.031 – 0.047 in.)

        **Minimum margin thickness:** 0.5 mm (0.020 in.)

        If the margin thickness is less than minimum, replace the valve.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM0181.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    4.  Check the valve overall length.

        **Standard overall length:**

        * Intake – 100.60 mm (3.9606 in.)
        * Exhaust – 100.45 mm (3.9547 in.)

        **Minimum overall length:**

        * Intake – 100.1 mm (3.941 in.)
        * Exhaust – 100.0 mm (3.937 in.)

        If the overall length is less than minimum, replace the valve.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM2534.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    5.  Check the surface of the valve stem tip for wear.

        If the valve stem tip is worn, resurface the tip with a grinder or replace the valve.

        !!! note "Notice"

            Do not grind off more than the minimum.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM0255.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

8.  [](){ #p-em-114 } Inspect and clean valve seats.

    1.  Using a 45° carbide cutter, resurface the valve seats. Remove only enough metal to clean the seats.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3300.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    2.  Check the valve seating position.

        Apply a thin coat of prussian blue (or white lead) to the valve face. Lightly press to the valve against the
        seat. Do not rotate the valve.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM0183_EM0635.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    3.  Check the valve face and seat for the following:

        * If blue appears 360° around the face, the valve is concentric. If not, replace the valve.
        * If blue appears 360° around the valve seat, the guide and face are concentric. If not, resurface the seat.
        * Check that the seat contact is in the middle of the valve face with the following width:

            1.0 – 1.4 mm (0.039 – 0.055 in.)

        If not, correct the valve seats as follows:

        1.  If the seating is too high on the valve face, use 30° and 45° cutters to correct the seat.

            <figure markdown="span">
              ![](images/EM0185.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
            </figure>

        2.  If the seating is too low on the valve face, use 75° and 45° cutters to correct the seat.

            <figure markdown="span">
              ![](images/EM0186.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
            </figure>

    4.  [](){ #p-em-115 } Hand-lap the valve and valve seat with an abrasive compound.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3303.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    5.  After hand-lapping, clean the valve and valve seat.

9.  Inspect valve springs.

    1.  Using a steel square, measure the squareness of the valve spring.

        **Maximum squareness:** 2.0 mm (0.075 in.)

        If squareness is greater than maximum, replace the valve spring.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM0988.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    2.  Using vernier calipers, measure the free length of the valve spring.

        **Free length:** 45.0 mm (1.772 in.)

        If the free length is not as specified, replace the valve spring.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM0801.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    3.  Using a spring tester, measure the tension of the valve spring at the specified installed length.

        **Installed tension:** 16.7 – 19.3 kg (36.8 – 42.5 lb, 164 – 189 N) at 34.7 mm (0.000 in.)

        If the installed tension is not as specified, replace the valve spring.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM0281.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

10. Inspect camshafts and bearings.

    1.  **Inspect camshaft for runout**

        1. Place the camshaft on V-blocks.
        2. Using a dial indicator, measure the circle runout at the center journal.

        **Maximum circle runout:** 0.04 mm (0.0016 in.)

        If the circle runout is greater than maximum, replace the camshaft.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM1628.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    2.  [](){ #p-em-116 } **Inspect cam lobes**

        Using a micrometer, measure the cam lobe height.

        **Standard cam lobe height:**

        * Intake – 35.310 – 35.410 mm (1.3902 – 1.3941 in.)
        * Exhaust – 35.560 – 35.660 mm (1.4000 – 1.4039 in.)

        **Minimum cam lobe height:**

        * Intake – 35.20 mm (1.3858 in.)
        * Exhaust – 35.45 mm (1.3957 in.)

        If the cam lobe height is greater than minimum, replace the camshaft.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM2011.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    3.  **Inspect camshaft journals**

        Using a micrometer, measure the journal diameter.

        **Journal diameter:** 26.959 – 26.975 mm (1.0614 – 1.0620 in.)

        If the journal diameter is not as specified, check the oil clearance.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM2538.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    4.  **Inspect camshaft bearings**

        Check the bearings for flaking and scoring.

        If the bearings are damaged, replace the bearing caps and cylinder head as a set.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM7780.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    5.  **Inspect camshaft gear spring**

        Using vernier calipers, measure the free distance between the spring end.

        **Free distance:** 22.5 – 22.9 mm (0.886 – 0.902 in.)

        If the free distance is not as specified, replace the gear spring.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3322.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    6.  **Inspect camshaft journal oil clearance**

        1. Clean the bearing caps and camshaft journals.
        2. Place the camshafts on the cylinder head.
        3.  Lay a strip of Plastigage across each of the camshaft journals.

            <figure markdown="span">
              ![](images/EM3371.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
            </figure>

        4.  [](){ #p-em-117 } Install the bearing caps. (See step 4 on pages EM-123 to 125)

            **Torque:** 190 kg-cm (14 ft-lb, 19 N·m)

            !!! note "Notice"

                Do not turn the camshaft.

            <figure markdown="span">
              ![](images/EM7524.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
            </figure>

        5.  Remove the bearing caps.

            <figure markdown="span">
              ![](images/EM7525.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
            </figure>

        6.  Measure the Plastigage at its widest point.

            **Standard oil clearance:** 0.025 – 0.062 mm (0.0010 – 0.0024 in.)

            **Maximum oil clearance:** 0.10 mm (0.0039 in.)

            If the oil clearance is greater than maximum, replace the camshaft. If necessary, replace the bearing
            caps and cylinder head as a set.

            <figure markdown="span">
              ![](images/EM3310.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
            </figure>

        7. Completely remove the Plastigage.

    7.  **Inspect camshaft thrust clearance**

        1. Install the camshafts. (See step 4 on pages EM-123 to 125)
        2. Using a dial indicator, measure the thrust clearance while moving the camshaft back and forth.

        **Standard thrust clearance:**

        * Intake – 0.045 – 0.100 mm (0.0018 – 0.0039 in.)
        * Exhaust – 0.030 – 0.085 mm (0.0012 – 0.0033 in.)

        **Maximum thrust clearance:**

        * Intake – 0.12 mm (0.0047 in.)
        * Exhaust – 0.10 mm (0.0039 in.)

        If the thrust clearance is greater than maximum, replace the camshaft. If necessary, replace the bearing
        caps and cylinder head as a set.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3307.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    8.  [](){ #p-em-118 } **Inspect camshaft gear backlash**

        1. Install the camshafts without installing the exhaust cam sub-gear. (See step 4 on page EM-123 to 125)
        2. Using a dial indicator, measure the backlash.

        **Standard backlash:** 0.020 – 0.200 mm (0.0008 – 0.0079 in.)

        **Maximum backlash:** 0.30 mm (0.0188 in.)

        If the backlash is greater than maximum, replace the camshafts.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3308.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

11. Inspect valve lifters and lifter bores.

    1.  Using a caliper gauge, measure the lifter bore diameter of the cylinder head.

        **Lifter bore diameter:** 28.000 – 28.021 mm (1.1024 – 1.1032 in.)

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3372.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    2.  Using a micrometer, measure the lifter diameter.

        **Lifter diameter:** 27.975 – 27.985 mm (1.1014 – 1.1018 in.)

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM2196.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    3.  Subtract the lifter diameter measurement from the lifter bore diameter measurement.

        **Standard oil clearance:** 0.015 – 0.046 mm (0.0005 – 0.0018 in.)

        **Maximum oil clearance:** 0.07 mm (0.0028 in.)

        If the oil clearance is greater than maximum, replace the lifter. If necessary, replace the cylinder head.

12. Inspect intake and exhaust manifolds.

    Using precision straight edge and feeler gauge, measure the surface contacting the cylinder head for warpage.

    **Maximum warpage:** 0.30 mm (0.0118 in.)

    If warpage is greater than maximum, replace the manifold.

    <figure markdown="span">
      ![](images/EM3324.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
    </figure>

4.  [](){ #p-em-123 } Install camshafts.

    !!! note "Notice"

        Since the thrust clearance of the camshaft is small, the camshaft must be kept level while it is being
        installed. If the camshaft is not kept level, the portion of the cylinder head receiving the shaft thrust may
        crack or be damaged, causing the camshaft to seize or break. To avoid this, the following steps should be
        carried out.

    **A. Install intake camshaft**

    1.  Apply MP grease to the thrust portion of the camshaft.
    2.  Place the intake camshaft at 80 – 115° BTDC of camshaft angle on the cylinder head.

        !!! tip "Hint"

            The above angle allows the No.1 and No.3 cylinder cam lobes of the intake camshaft to push their valve
            lifters evenly.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3520.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    3.  Apply seal packing to the No.1 bearing cap as shown.

        **Seal packing:** Part No. `08826-00080` or equivalent

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3373.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    4.  Install the bearing caps in their proper locations.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM7727.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    5.  Apply a light coat of engine oil on the threads and under the heads of the bearing cap bolts.
    6.  Install and uniformly tighten the ten bearing cap bolts in several passes in the sequence shown.

        **Torque:** 190 kg-cm (14 ft-lb, 19 N·m)

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3280.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    7.  [](){ #p-em-124 } Apply MP grease to a new oil seal lip.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM0050.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    8.  Using SST, tap in the oil seal.

        **SST** `09223-46011`

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3253.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    **B. Install exhaust camshaft**

    1.  Set the knock pin of the intake camshaft at 10 – 45° BTDC of camshaft angle.

        !!! tip "Hint"

            The above angle allows the No.2 and No.4 cylinder cam lobes of the exhaust camshaft to push their valve
            lifters evenly.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3254.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    2.  Apply MP grease to the thrust portion of the camshaft.
    3.  Engage the exhaust camshaft gear to the intake camshaft gear by matching the timing marks on each gear.
    4.  Roll down the exhaust camshaft onto the bearing journals while engaging gears with each other.

        !!! note "Notice"

            There are also assembly reference marks on each gear as shown in the illustration. Do not use these
            marks.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM3255.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    5.  Turn the intake camshaft clockwise or counterclockwise little by little until the exhaust camshaft sits in the
        bearing journals evenly without rocking the camshaft on the bearing journals.

        !!! note "Notice"

            It is very important to replace the camshaft in the bearing journals evenly while tightening bearing caps
            in the subsequent steps.

    6.  Install the bearing caps in their proper location.

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM7665.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

    7.  [](){ #p-em-125 } Apply a light coat of engine oil on the threads and under the heads of bearing cap bolts.
    8.  Install and uniformly tighten the ten bearing cap bolts in several passes, in the sequence shown.

        **Torque:** 190 kg-cm (14 ft-lb, 19 N·m)

        <figure markdown="span">
          ![](images/EM7664.webp#illustration){ width="400px" }
        </figure>

        <figure markdown="span">
